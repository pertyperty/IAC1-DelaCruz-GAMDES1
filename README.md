## Course Description
> Game Design and Development is a project-based course for Bachelor of Science in Computer Science students that introduces the theory and practice of designing and building playable games. The course covers formal game elements, the MDA (Mechanics-Dynamics-Aesthetics) framework, core gameplay loops, level design, systems and balance, narrative design, accessibility, and professional practice, alongside hands-on implementation in the Godot engine across mixed 2D and 3D projects. Students work through interactive lecture activities and studio-style lab sessions that mirror professional game studio workflows, from concept through prototyping, playtesting, and postmortem reflection. The course culminates in a complete game design document, a playable prototype, and a professional postmortem that evidence the student's capacity to design, build, and critically evaluate interactive computing solutions.

C. In your own words, what is the relationship between a node, a scene, and the scene tree?
Node is a simple building block that contains the assets. Scene contains a compilation of nodes that makes up a single 'object' in the game like, is stored as .tscn file, and can be cloned to make multiple scenes. A scene tree is basically a compilation of scenes, which is one of the major building blocks of the game.
